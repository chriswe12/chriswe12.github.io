Autonomous Drone Perception Stack

SPRIND Fully Autonomous Flight Challenge 2.0 · Jetson AGX Orin · YOLO · Qwen3-VL · GroundingDINO

During my internship at Avientus, I worked in a three-person team on the autonomous drone stack for the SPRIND Fully Autonomous Flight Challenge 2.0. I led the perception work and also contributed to visual-inertial SLAM with OKVIS2, path-planning and navigation integration, sensor bring-up, ROS 2 integration, and real-hardware testing.

My work covered training and fine-tuning vision models, building the perception pipelines for both challenge tasks, and deploying them on an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin under real-time constraints. I also helped integrate cameras and collision-avoidance radars over CAN bus, design and 3D-print sensor mounts, and connect the perception outputs to the rest of the autonomy stack.

Both tasks required the drone to operate without GNSS, a preloaded map, or a known starting pose. It had to explore an unfamiliar environment and turn semantic instructions into targets that planning and navigation could use.

GNSS-Denied Last-Mile Delivery

For the last-mile delivery task, the drone first had to find a requested house number and then locate a specified object nearby. I collected and annotated a dataset and fine-tuned YOLO to detect house-number signs in the live camera feed. DeepSORT tracked the signs across frames to filter out unstable detections. Once a stable sign was found, Qwen3-VL read the digits; if they matched the requested address, the drone began searching the surrounding area for the object.

Object search followed the same approach. GroundingDINO detected candidates for the requested object, DeepSORT tracked them in real time, and Qwen3-VL verified that a stable candidate actually matched the request. Running detection and tracking continuously while using the slower VLM only for verification allowed the complete pipeline to run locally on the Jetson AGX Orin before passing a confirmed target to navigation and landing.

GNSS-Denied Search And Rescue

For search and rescue, I developed a pipeline that used YOLO segmentation and DeepSORT tracking to detect and follow people, together with VLM verification to identify a person from a visual description. The same combination of a faster real-time model, tracking, and a higher-level semantic check made the system both responsive and flexible.

After identifying the correct person, the system combined stereo depth with visual-inertial pose estimates from OKVIS2 to provide a stable target position to the planner. I integrated this perception output with the drone's exploration, navigation, and person-following behavior.

The hardest part was making the full perception system run reliably on an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin while integrating with the real drone stack. That included model deployment, timing, networking, ROS 2 integration, and ensuring that data arrived without drops. The team advanced from stage 1 to stage 2 and received a EUR 325k award.
